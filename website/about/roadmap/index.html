What is built.
What is next.

Smart Roots LIMS is developed in the open. This roadmap lists what the project has built, what it is working on next and where it is heading after that.

It carries no dates. Work is grouped by what a laboratory needs and ordered by what stands between a laboratory and daily use. The order can change when laboratories say otherwise.

The journey
is in place.

A sample can travel from receipt to an amended report with its custody, QC, review and release on record. These areas are implemented and covered by automated checks on synthetic data. The project has not yet made a stable release.

Next

What daily work
still needs.

Six areas stand between the current software and a laboratory that runs its routine work in it. They are listed in the order the project intends to take them.

  1. A first stable release

    Laboratories are asked to evaluate with synthetic data. They need a version they can install, check and upgrade.

    • Versioned releases with upgrade notes and tested migrations
    • Holding-time limits per analyte, where one method covers analytes with different limits
    Back to the roadmap

  2. Calculated results at the bench

    Wet chemistry and soil results are still worked out on paper or in a spreadsheet and typed in as final values. The readings behind a result belong in the record.

    • Worksheets for BOD, solids by weight and titrations that keep each reading
    • Percent moisture and dry-weight correction for soil
    • Dilution and preparation factors applied to results and reporting limits
    • Rounding and significant-figure rules set by the laboratory
    • Versioned calculations, so a reported value can be reproduced
    Back to the roadmap

  3. Instrument data

    Results load today from CSV and TSV files that an operator maps. Laboratories expect instrument exports to load as they are, with the raw file kept beside the batch.

    • Import layouts checked against real exports from common instrument types, such as ICP, discrete analyzers, ion chromatography and TOC
    • Excel workbooks
    • Raw files stored with the batch, in storage sized for them
    • Run lists exported for the instrument
    • Calibration recorded in the run, with curves and verification standards linked to results
    Back to the roadmap

  4. Invoicing

    A commercial laboratory bills for its work. Price lists and quotes exist, invoices do not.

    • Invoices built from quotes, price lists and the work received
    • Billing contact, purchase order and payment terms taken from the client and project records
    • Export to accounting software, which stays the ledger
    • An optional account hold shown at receipt and at release
    Back to the roadmap

  5. Notifications and client results

    The application sends no notifications today, and the portal shows status and report files. Clients want to be told, and to work with their numbers.

    • Email when samples arrive, when a receipt problem needs a decision and when a report is issued or amended
    • Reminders for staff, such as holding times at risk and work returned from review
    • Result tables in the portal, with download
    • Trends by sampling location, with results marked against limits the client supplies
    Back to the roadmap

  6. Electronic deliverables

    Many clients and agencies take data as a file in their own format, and accept it only against their own specification. One exploratory export exists.

    • A configurable CSV and Excel deliverable for client templates
    • The EQuIS-style four-file deliverable with reference values, passed through a named recipient's checker
    • Report packages with a QC summary, chosen per order
    • Permit summaries for discharge monitoring reports: averages, maximums and loadings. The permittee still files the report.
    Back to the roadmap

Later

Depth
and breadth.

Planned after the work above. The order here is looser, and a laboratory that needs one of these can move it forward.

  • Quality system records

    Assessors look past sample data to the laboratory's whole quality system. Equipment, materials, training and nonconformance are recorded today. These complete the set.

    • Proficiency testing: studies, reported results, scores and follow-up
    • Controlled documents: SOP versions linked to methods, with read acknowledgement
    • Internal audits, findings, complaints and management review
    • Checks at the point of use that a laboratory can turn on: a current demonstration of capability, equipment in calibration, lots within expiry
    • Method validation, measurement uncertainty and ongoing MDL verification
    Back to the roadmap

  • Management view

    Work queues show what is waiting. Managers also need to see how the laboratory is doing.

    • Turnaround, on-time delivery and backlog by stage
    • Due dates worked out from turnaround terms and the laboratory's working calendar
    • Holding-time misses and QC failure rates by method
    • Workload by method and analyst
    • Audit trail search by person, record and date
    Back to the roadmap

  • Drinking water programs

    Drinking water compliance results count only from a laboratory certified for the method and analyte, and many states take them electronically.

    • A register of certified or accredited scope per program, with results outside it marked
    • Public water system, facility and sampling point identifiers
    • Compliance sample types, such as routine and repeat samples
    • Electronic reporting to a state agency, starting with the format of EPA's Compliance Monitoring Data Portal
    Back to the roadmap

  • Microbiology

    Coliform and E. coli testing is routine in water and wastewater laboratories, and its results are counts or presence, not concentrations.

    • Presence or absence, most probable number and plate count results
    • Incubation start and read times checked against the method's window
    • Media, sterility and incubator temperature records
    Back to the roadmap

  • Sampling and field work

    Much of a laboratory's work repeats on a permit schedule and starts before the cooler arrives.

    • Recurring sampling plans, with expected and missed collections visible
    • Sampling locations kept per client and project
    • A bottle catalog, kit orders and pre-printed labels
    • Field measurements recorded with the sample, such as pH, temperature, residual chlorine and depth
    • Capture on a phone or tablet in the field
    Back to the roadmap

  • Trace organics and PFAS

    The first method set covers metals, nutrients and solids. Organic methods bring their own QC, and PFAS work adds isotope dilution at parts-per-trillion levels.

    • Surrogates, internal standards and tentatively identified compounds
    • Isotope dilution with labeled-standard recoveries, as EPA Method 1633A uses
    • Summed analytes, and branched and linear isomers
    Back to the roadmap

  • Integrations and scale

    A laboratory system works beside other systems and grows with the laboratory.

    • An API and webhooks for status, results and reports
    • Single sign-on, a sign-in policy the laboratory sets and a signature step at release
    • Background jobs for long imports and report runs
    • Stronger tamper evidence for the audit trail and issued reports
    Back to the roadmap

What shaped
the order.

Three things: the public rules a US environmental laboratory works under, what laboratories ask for when they choose a system, and what the project's own documents list as unfinished. Sources were checked in November 2026.

What laboratories ask for
Public requests for proposals from water utility laboratories list instrument interfaces, calibration records, proficiency testing, data migration and acceptance testing among their requirements. A commercial laboratory also has to bill. That puts bench work, instruments, invoicing and deliverables first.
Accreditation records
Laboratories accredited to the TNI standard or ISO/IEC 17025 keep records for proficiency testing, document control, audits, complaints and management review. The roadmap adds those records. It does not reproduce either standard.
Permit reporting
Clean Water Act methods set holding times, preservation and QC elements (40 CFR Part 136). Permittees file their discharge monitoring reports electronically (NPDES Electronic Reporting Rule), and the laboratory supplies the results, so the plan is a summary a permittee can file from.
Deliverables
A recipient accepts an electronic deliverable only against its own current specification and reference values. Formats are added one recipient at a time and are called supported only after that recipient's checker passes them.
Drinking water
Compliance samples must be analyzed by a certified laboratory (40 CFR 141.28). EPA's Compliance Monitoring Data Portal lets laboratories report to state agencies electronically.
PFAS
EPA Method 1633A measures 40 PFAS in water, solids, biosolids and tissue. Its data differs enough from metals and nutrients to be its own item.

Help set
the order.

Tell the project what your laboratory needs, or share an instrument export layout filled with invented values. Laboratory feedback moves items on this page.

Smart Roots LIMS supports laboratory records and quality systems. It does not certify compliance or accreditation, and nothing on this page is a commitment to a date.
